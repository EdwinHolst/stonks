## Business model

Sustainion is an **acquisition-driven industrial holding company** that buys and operates small Swedish tech-and-manufacturing businesses focused on sustainability and environmental themes. It is an active, long-term owner with a stated goal of profitable growth through close collaboration with subsidiaries.

Revenue is entirely generated by the subsidiaries — the parent is purely a holding vehicle.

## Subsidiary breakdown and margin analysis

### Why EBITDA margins are thin: the Skårebo effect

The group's stated EBITDA target is **12%**. FY2025 came in at **10.0%**. The gap is almost entirely explained by one business: Skårebo Energi.

Skårebo is a **pure wholesale distributor** of EV charging equipment — it buys from manufacturers and resells into the Nordic and European installer/contractor market. Distribution businesses structurally carry thin margins. Skårebo's standalone FY2024 net margin was ~3.7% (9 MSEK profit on 244 MSEK revenue); EBITDA margin is estimated at ~7–9%. When it was acquired (November 2024) and became roughly **55% of group revenue**, it mechanically dragged the blended margin toward its own profile.

The proof: the legacy businesses (ex-Skårebo) ran at **12.3% EBITDA** in FY2024 — exactly at target. H1 2024 margin was 14.2%. The moment Skårebo consolidated (Q4 2024), margin fell to 8%, and FY2025 settled at 10%.

### Cost structure observations

From the income statement (FY2025):
- **Gross margin: 29%** (155.7 MSEK gross profit on 537.8 MSEK revenue). This is compressed by Skårebo's pass-through distribution model; legacy manufacturing/tech businesses likely carry 40–60%+ gross margins.
- **D&A: 15.4 MSEK** (~2.9% of revenue), primarily depreciation of acquired fixed assets and right-of-use assets. Low relative to revenue because most acquired businesses are asset-light services.
- **Operating expenses (ex-COGS): 119.7 MSEK** (~22% of revenue) — overheads, SG&A, and central holding costs.
- **Interest expense: 8.5 MSEK** (~1.6% of revenue). Modest but meaningful given thin absolute profits.
- **Acquisition costs: 3.1 MSEK** in restructuring/M&A charges (FY2025), down from prior years — consistent with digesting recent deals.

### Path to 12% EBITDA target

Three levers:
1. **Skårebo margin improvement** — even a 2 pp improvement in Skårebo's EBITDA margin (to ~9–11%) would add ~6 MSEK to group EBITDA, moving the blended margin to ~11%.
2. **Revenue mix shift** — as Ströman Maskin (10.9% EBIT) and EHC Teknik (growing internationally) scale, higher-margin businesses become a larger share of the mix.
3. **Organic growth in legacy businesses** — legacy units grew organically at ~26% in FY2025 on a small base, which provides operating leverage on the fixed overhead structure.

## Summary

Sustainion is a small-cap Swedish industrial acquirer (market cap ~390 MSEK) that has grown revenue roughly 8× from 68 MSEK in FY 2021 to 538 MSEK in FY 2025, entirely through M&A. The two most recent acquisitions — Skårebo Energi (EV charging, Nov 2024) and Ströman Maskin (road/waste machinery, 2025) — account for most of the revenue jump from 189 MSEK to 538 MSEK between FY 2024 and FY 2025.

Profitability is thin but improving: net margin expanded from ~2% (FY 2024) to ~4% (FY 2025) and FCF turned positive (19 MSEK) in FY 2025. The balance sheet carries 103 MSEK of debt against 157 MSEK equity (D/E ~0.65), reflecting acquisition financing.

The investment thesis is essentially a bet on management's capital-allocation ability — can they keep finding attractively priced industrial niche businesses and integrate them without over-leveraging the balance sheet.
